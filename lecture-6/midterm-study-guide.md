# Midterm Study Guide

Handed out in Week 6 (Sat Jun 13, 2026). Read it the day you get it, not the night before the exam.

## Logistics

- **Date:** Saturday Jun 27, 2026 (Week 8), in class.
- **Length:** 90 minutes, written.
- **Scope:** Lectures 1 through 6 only. Everything we covered from "Why Big Data" through "Spark Foundations."
- **Not on the exam:** the new material introduced in the Week 7 review session. That content is for the second half of the course and will not be tested here.
- **Style:** the exam is mostly theoretical. There is little to no coding. It tests whether you understand the concepts and can reason about *why*, not whether you can write a Spark job from memory.

## What to expect

The exam is built mostly from short questions, with a few longer ones. By rough weight:

- **Multiple choice** — pick the best answer. Distractors are plausible, so read all options.
- **Short-answer questions** — a sentence or two to define a term, state a trade-off, or say why something matters. These make up the bulk of the exam.
- **Descriptive questions** — a few longer prompts where you explain a concept, compare two approaches, or reason through a scenario in a short paragraph.
- **Diagramming** — sketch or label a diagram by hand (for example, an HDFS read path, a Spark DAG with its shuffle boundary, or a star schema). Neatness is not graded; correctness of the parts and their relationships is.

There are no trick questions, no arithmetic for its own sake, and nothing from outside the lectures.

## How to prepare

- Re-read the notes for Lectures 1 through 6, then close them and explain each topic out loud.
- Re-walk Assignments 1 and 2. The exam rewards the same reasoning.
- Practice the diagrams by hand: the HDFS read/write path, a Spark DAG with its shuffle boundary, OLTP versus OLAP, and a star schema. If you can redraw it from memory, you can label it on the exam.
- Bring nothing but yourself unless told otherwise; this is a closed-notes written exam.
